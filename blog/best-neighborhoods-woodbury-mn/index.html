Woodbury, MN Neighborhood Guide: Which Area Is Right for You?

A practical look at five Woodbury neighborhoods buyers tend to consider, what each one actually offers, and how to figure out which one fits the way you want to live.

Gian "G" Visciglio, REALTOR®
Updated September 11, 2026  ·  Keller Williams Premier Realty  ·  MN License #41031450

If you're thinking about buying a home in Woodbury, you might assume the hardest part is finding the right house.

Sometimes it is.

But there's another decision that can be just as important: where in Woodbury do you actually want to live?

Woodbury is a big city, and the neighborhoods don't all feel the same. Some areas have newer homes, community pools, and HOA amenities. Others have older homes, bigger trees, and more established neighborhoods. Some are closer to shopping and major roads. Others feel quieter and more tucked away.

And two homes with the same Woodbury address can have very different price tags, layouts, lot sizes, and overall feel.

So rather than trying to tell you which neighborhood is "the best," here's a practical look at some of the areas buyers tend to consider and what you should know about each one.

First, a Quick Look at Woodbury

Woodbury has grown significantly over the past few decades, and that growth is one reason the city has such a mix of housing.

Generally speaking, you'll find newer development toward the eastern side of the city, more established neighborhoods through central Woodbury, and some of the city's older homes and mature landscaping toward the west.

The city also has an extensive trail system connecting neighborhoods with parks, schools, and other parts of the community.

That means your choice isn't just about the house. It's also about how you want to live in the house.

Do you want a newer home with fewer immediate projects? A bigger yard? A neighborhood pool? Mature trees? A shorter drive to shopping? A quieter street?

Those questions can narrow the search pretty quickly.

Stonemill Farms

If you've spent much time looking at Woodbury homes, you've probably come across Stonemill Farms.

It's one of Woodbury's better-known master-planned communities, with newer single-family homes, community amenities, trails, and a strong neighborhood feel. The community was largely developed from the 2000s onward.

What buyers tend to like:

Newer construction Community amenities Neighborhood trails and green space Larger homes with modern layouts Strong sense of community

Things to consider: The amenities come with HOA costs, and you're generally paying a premium compared with some of Woodbury's older neighborhoods.

If you want a move-in-ready home and like the idea of having neighborhood amenities close by, Stonemill Farms is worth a look. If your priority is getting the most house for your money, there may be better options elsewhere.

Dancing Waters

Dancing Waters has a different feel from a typical newer subdivision.

You'll find custom and semi-custom homes, ponds and water features, larger lots in some areas, and more variety from one home to the next.

What buyers tend to like:

More individual-looking homes Larger lots in some sections Water views and ponds Quiet residential feel Higher-end homes and finishes

Things to consider: Custom homes can be harder to compare from one property to another. One house may have a completely different floor plan, lot, or level of finish than the house next door.

If you're looking for something that feels a little less cookie-cutter, Dancing Waters is one of the Woodbury areas I'd put on the list.

Bailey Lake

Bailey Lake is a good example of why looking beyond the house itself matters.

The area around the lake has a more established, park-like feel in some sections, with mature trees, larger lots, and access to outdoor recreation.

What buyers tend to like:

Mature trees and landscaping Larger lots in some sections Access to the lake and surrounding parks Established neighborhood feel Less of the "brand new subdivision" look

Things to consider: Not every home near Bailey Lake has the same level of access or views, so don't assume that being "near the lake" means you're getting a lake-oriented property. Some of the older homes may also need updates.

For buyers who care more about the setting and lot than having the newest finishes, this area can be worth exploring.

Eagle Valley

If value is high on your list, don't automatically skip the older neighborhoods.

Eagle Valley has a mix of older homes, including some dating back to the 1980s and 1990s. That means you may find homes that aren't as polished as some of Woodbury's newer neighborhoods, but that can also create opportunities.

What buyers tend to like:

Typically lower entry price than newer neighborhoods Established landscaping Opportunity to update a home over time Access to Woodbury's parks, trails, schools, and shopping

Things to consider: You need to look at the whole house, not just the kitchen. An older home may have an older roof, furnace, windows, electrical components, plumbing, or other systems that aren't obvious from the listing photos. This is where a good inspection becomes especially important.

If you're comfortable buying a home that needs some work, Eagle Valley and similar older areas can sometimes give you more flexibility than chasing a fully updated home in one of Woodbury's newer developments.

Royal Oaks

Royal Oaks is interesting because there is more variety within the same general area.

You'll find both townhomes and single-family homes, which creates a wider range of prices and maintenance levels.

What buyers tend to like:

Different housing options Townhomes for lower-maintenance living Single-family options without the highest price ranges Access to Woodbury amenities

Things to consider: If you're looking at a townhome, the HOA becomes an important part of the decision. Don't just look at the monthly fee. Look at what it covers, the association's financial health, recent assessments, rules, and what you'll actually be responsible for maintaining.

For someone who wants to stay in Woodbury but isn't set on a traditional single-family home, Royal Oaks can give you more options.

What About the Rest of Woodbury?

These aren't the only neighborhoods worth considering.

One of the things I like about Woodbury is that you can find very different types of homes without leaving the city.

There are older neighborhoods with mature trees and established streets. There are newer developments with modern floor plans and amenities. There are areas closer to major shopping and highways, and quieter pockets that feel further removed from the busier parts of town.

That's why I wouldn't recommend choosing a neighborhood based solely on a list like this.

I'd start with what matters most to you.

What Matters Most to You?

If you want newer construction

Start by looking at some of Woodbury's newer developments. You'll generally get more modern layouts, newer mechanical systems, and fewer immediate renovation projects. The trade-off is often a higher purchase price and, in some communities, an HOA.

If you want mature trees and established neighborhoods

Look toward some of Woodbury's older areas. You may give up some of the newer floor plans and finishes, but you can get larger trees, established landscaping, and a neighborhood that has had decades to mature.

If you want the most house for your budget

Don't limit yourself to the newest neighborhoods. An older home that needs cosmetic work can sometimes give you more square footage or a larger lot for the same money as a smaller, newer home. Just make sure you're separating cosmetic updates from expensive maintenance items. New flooring is one thing. A 25-year-old roof, furnace, windows, and air conditioner are another.

If you want amenities

Look closely at master-planned communities and neighborhoods with pools, clubhouses, trails, or other shared amenities. Just remember that those amenities aren't complimentary. HOA dues are part of the cost of owning the home and should be included in your monthly budget.

If schools are a major priority

Start with the specific address, not just the city or neighborhood name. School boundaries can change, and some Woodbury addresses may have different school assignments depending on where they're located. Always verify the current assignment for the specific property you're considering.

What About Cottage Grove, Lake Elmo, or Oakdale?

You don't have to stop your search at the Woodbury city limits.

Cottage Grove, Lake Elmo, and Oakdale all offer something different and can make sense depending on your priorities. The better way to look at it is by trade-off rather than simply ranking the communities.

  • Cottage Grove: Worth considering if you want to stretch your budget while staying in the South Washington County area.
  • Lake Elmo: Worth a look if you want more space, larger lots, or a more rural feel while staying close to Woodbury.
  • Oakdale: Worth considering if commute and affordability are more important to you than having a Woodbury address.

Sometimes the right answer isn't a different neighborhood in Woodbury. It's a different city five or ten minutes away.

The Neighborhood Matters, But So Does the Street

This is probably the biggest thing I'd tell someone who is new to Woodbury: don't buy the neighborhood. Buy the specific property.

Two homes in the same neighborhood can have completely different experiences.

One might back up to a trail or pond. Another might back up to a busy road. One might have a flat backyard with great privacy. Another might have drainage issues. One street might be quiet. Another might have significantly more traffic.

And a house that looks like a great deal may have a reason it's priced below everything around it.

This is where seeing the property in person matters.

How I'd Narrow Down Your Woodbury Search

Instead of starting with a list of neighborhoods, I'd start with five questions.

1. What's your budget?

Not just your maximum purchase price. What's a monthly payment you're actually comfortable with after taxes, insurance, HOA dues, and everything else?

2. How important is the age of the home?

Would you rather have a newer home with fewer projects, or an older home with more character and potentially more room for your budget?

3. How much yard do you want?

Lot size can vary quite a bit across Woodbury.

4. How important are amenities?

Pool, clubhouse, trails, parks, shopping, and proximity to major roads can all matter differently depending on how you live.

5. What are you willing to compromise on?

This is usually the question that matters most. You may not get the biggest house, newest construction, best lot, lowest price, and perfect location all at once. Knowing which two or three matter most makes the search much easier.

The Bottom Line

There isn't one "best" neighborhood in Woodbury.

There's the best fit for you.

If you want newer construction and amenities, some of the newer master-planned communities may make sense. If you want mature trees and an established neighborhood, look at some of the older parts of the city. If budget is your biggest concern, don't automatically rule out an older home that needs some updating.

And if you're not finding what you want in Woodbury, take a look at the communities around it. You may find that a slightly different location gives you more of what you're actually looking for.

The goal isn't to find the neighborhood with the best reputation. It's to find the neighborhood, street, and house that make sense for how you actually want to live.
